RTT 18-week Diversion: a study in metric gaming

View source on GitHub

An animated, fictional waiting list from today to 1 April 2028, under three capacity positions (surplus, balance, deficit), each run twice: once treating patients in the ordinary way throughout, and once where, for the final 18 weeks, capacity is spent only on patients who will have waited 18+ weeks on the measurement date. Pure JavaScript, deterministic, unit-tested.

A gentle note on why this exists. The second row of charts models a tactic the author does not think is right: deliberately withholding treatment from patients who would still count as "under 18 weeks" on 1 April, so that the headline RTT figure on that one day looks better. No extra patient is treated. The same capacity is simply pointed at the metric rather than at patients in a sensible clinical and chronological order, and the list the morning after is exactly as long. The point of showing it is to make the mechanism, and its hangover, visible.

1 · Inputs

The list starts as an exponential (random-order steady state) sized by Little's law for the referral rate and the chosen % under 18 weeks. Each week: everyone ages a week, referrals arrive, then capacity removes patients. Load is referrals ÷ capacity, so 1.05 means 5% more referrals than clock stops each week.

2 · The list, week by week

Each chart is the census by weeks waited (0 to 104+). The dashed rule marks 18 weeks. In the bottom row, during the final 18 weeks the hatched block is the cohort that capacity is forbidden to touch: patients who will still be under 18 weeks on 1 April. Capacity spills over to them only if the eligible cohort runs out.

5 · Method & caveats

  • Weekly steps, continuous (fractional) patient counts, no randomness: the same inputs always give the same picture. Referrals arrive at a constant rate; capacity is a constant number of removals per week, referrals ÷ load.
  • Removal order mixes two rules. The random share is removed in proportion to each weeks-waited bin (the M/M/1 "random order" case that gives the exponential shape). The remainder is removed longest-waiting-first. At 100% random the list keeps its exponential shape; at 0% it becomes a flat block with a hard edge. The slider sets the mix for all six runs.
  • Diversion rule: in a step with r weeks still to go before 1 April, a patient who has waited i weeks will have waited i + r on the day, and may be treated only if i + r ≥ 18. The rule bites only in the last 18 weeks and is switched off after 1 April. It applies to all capacity, including the random share, so clinical urgency is not modelled as an exemption.
  • The starting shape is exponential whatever the removal order, so the first weeks of every run show the list reshaping towards the steady state of the chosen policy. That transient is real behaviour of the model, not a bug, but it is why the balance run's % under 18 moves even though its size does not.
  • Based on the queueing framing in Fong, House, Walton et al. (2022), Understanding Waiting List Pressures, and the sibling Waiting Lists app. Engine tests: node tests/engine.test.mjs.
